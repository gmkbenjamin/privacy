Tailpaw Privacy Policy

Effective 7 November 2026 · Part of the SmartStay Access privacy policy

Tailpaw connects your own devices to each other, using the networking engine from Tailscale's open-source tailcat project. This policy covers the Tailpaw app for iPhone and iPad.

Summary

Tailpaw collects nothing. There is no account, no analytics, no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDK. Nothing is sent to the developer. The app talks to the devices you connect it to, to Tailscale's public relay servers, which help those devices find and reach each other, and to tailcat.dev for the list of those servers. A web page you open from one of your devices can also load parts of itself from other websites, as it would in any browser.

Data we collect

None. The developer receives no data from this app: no usage data, no diagnostics, no crash reports, no identifiers.

Data stored on your device

All of the following stays on your device, and Tailpaw never uploads it. Your device's own backups, to iCloud or to a computer, include the app's files and settings as they do any app's; Keychain items are covered below the table.

SSH passwords are asked for each time and never stored. ThisDeviceOnly means the Keychain items never move to another device, even through a backup.

Deleting the app removes your files and settings, but iOS keeps an app's Keychain items after the app is deleted, and reinstalling Tailpaw finds them again. To remove them, use Erase keys and connections in Settings before deleting the app. It removes every Keychain item Tailpaw keeps, and the cookies and site data of web pages and of Safari within the app; Tailpaw then makes one new client key, as it does when first opened, which no device has been given.

Data sent off your device

  • To the devices you connect to: what you choose to send or open (files, terminal sessions, file browsing, web pages, and forwarded ports), end-to-end encrypted between your device and the other one.
  • To Tailscale's public relay servers (DERP): they help your devices find each other, and carry the traffic when no direct path is available. They see your device's IP address and the other device's, and encrypted packets they cannot read. They are run by Tailscale Inc. The list of relays comes from tailcat.dev unless you set your own, and measuring the fastest region sends test messages to them.
  • Directly between your devices: when a direct path is possible, your devices connect to each other directly, and each sees the other's IP address.
  • To an SSH server you sign in to: your username, and your password if you sign in with one.
  • To other websites, from a web page you open: a page one of your devices serves can load images, fonts, scripts and frames from other websites. They load directly, as they would in any browser, so those sites see your device's IP address.

Links to other websites open in Safari within the app, only after you confirm. Links to this device's own addresses, where Tailpaw's forwards listen, don't open there, whether they name an address or a name that resolves to one when the link is offered. Safari within the app follows redirects on its own, so a website that redirects to one of those addresses can still reach it, as can a name that resolves to one only once Safari loads it. A web page can't load anything from this device's own addresses except its own port, so cookies stay with the server that set them. That block goes by address: a page that loads from a name resolving to this device is not stopped, and can reach Tailpaw's other forwards, but sends none of those cookies.

Tracking

Tailpaw does not track you, does not collect data for advertising, and shares nothing with data brokers. Its privacy manifest declares no data collection and no tracking.

Children

Tailpaw is not directed at children, and it collects no data from anyone.
